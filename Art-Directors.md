# Curitopia --- Art Direction & Experience Principles

**Document:** `Art-Directors.md`\
**Audience:** Cursor and anyone implementing visual or interaction
changes to Curitopia\
**Purpose:** Establish a durable art-direction brief to guide
improvements across the product, one phase at a time.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Product ambition

> **Curitopia should feel like a place where people fall in love with
> discovering things they love and exploring the things they already
> love.**

Curitopia helps people discover, investigate and develop relationships
with the things that fascinate them.

It is not simply a question-and-answer tool, a productivity dashboard or
a conventional learning application. It is a personal environment for
curiosity: somewhere people can follow an idea, notice what attracts
them, collect concepts, make connections and understand where they have
agency.

The emotional foundation is:

> **Make people feel that their curiosity is worth caring about.**

Not because it is academically important, commercially useful or
productive, but simply because it belongs to them.

## 2. The experience in one sentence

Curitopia takes a person from an initial interest, through open
exploration and personal selection, into reflection, connection and a
clearer understanding of what they can influence or control.

### Phase 1 --- Home: Discover what you love

The Home screen is the user's personal garden of curiosities. It
contains the overarching subjects they want to investigate.

Its jobs are to: - Remind users of subjects they already love
exploring. - Make returning to an existing curiosity feel welcoming and
natural. - Make starting a new curiosity feel like planting a seed. -
Leave room for the discovery of entirely new interests.

Emotional message:

> *There's always something wonderful to discover here.*

### Phase 2 --- Question Garden: Follow your curiosity

This is the primary environment for asking questions about the current
subject.

The user should feel free to ask anything: relevant, irrelevant,
repeated, unusual, half-formed or surprising questions. The aim is to
encourage a state of flow, not to police the user's thinking.

Questions that seem unrelated may later prove meaningful. Relevance,
duplication and organisation can be considered during later reflection
or curation. Do not interrupt the user's thinking with premature
judgement.

Emotional message:

> *You can wonder about absolutely anything.*

### Phase 3 --- Visualise: Discover what attracts you

The user sees questions from their exploration and chooses the ones they
want to give visual form to. This is an expression of personal
attention, not a test of importance or a requirement to process every
question.

The user chooses a memorable **two-word title** for a visualised
concept. The title acts as a compact cognitive handle for recalling the
idea.

Unselected questions are not failures or discarded ideas. A session in
which the user chooses only one question can be entirely successful.

Emotional message:

> *Something about this idea has caught my attention.*

### Phase 4 --- Gallery: Appreciate what you've discovered

The Gallery is a personal collection of visualised interests. Each
concept has its artwork, two-word title and original question.

The user browses, revisits and develops a clearer understanding of
ideas. The experience should feel like wandering through a personal
gallery or cabinet of wonders, not managing a database.

The artwork and title should work together to make each concept
recognisable and memorable.

Emotional message:

> *Look at all these wonderful things I've discovered.*

### Phase 5 --- Compare: Discover connections

The user selects two concepts and views them side by side. Their
similarities, differences or tensions may reveal a relationship that
neither concept made obvious on its own.

A comparison may stimulate another question and open a new avenue of
exploration. It does not have to produce an insight every time.

Preserve the individuality of each concept. The space and relationship
between them are meaningful; do not automatically merge the two into one
idea.

Emotional message:

> *I wonder what happens when I put these ideas together.*

### Phase 6 --- Experience: Understand and find your agency

An Experience is associated with an overarching curiosity. It provides a
persistent place for reflective conversation, supported by AI that
understands the context accumulated throughout the journey.

The AI should be able to draw on the user's original curiosity,
questions, selected concepts, comparisons and previous thinking.
Conversations should feel continuous, not like starting a new chatbot
session every time.

The Experience helps the user distinguish:

-   **Concern --- FAR:** What matters to me, but whose outcome I cannot
    determine?
-   **Influence --- MIDDLE:** What can I shape or contribute to, without
    guaranteeing the outcome?
-   **Control --- NEAR:** What can I personally choose, do, practise or
    manage?

The purpose is not to dismiss things outside the user's control. It is
to help them recognise where their energy and agency can make a
difference while remaining open to wonder.

The **North Star** is a personal guiding statement describing how the
user wants to approach the subject. It can evolve as their understanding
changes.

The conversation is open-ended. The user describes the situation in
their own words; there is no prescribed sequence of questions to
complete.

Emotional message:

> *I understand this better, and I know where I can make a difference.*

## 4. The visual metaphor: a garden that helps explain the product

The garden metaphor is not merely decoration. It should help users
intuitively understand what is happening as their curiosity develops.

Use the following vocabulary consistently where it helps communicate
meaning:

  Motif      Meaning
  ---------- ---------------------------------------------
  Seed       A new question or curiosity
  Seedling   A question that has attracted attention
  Growth     An idea being developed and understood
  Branch     A connection or a new avenue of exploration
  Blossom    A developed concept worth appreciating
  Garden     The user's accumulated world of curiosity

These are guiding metaphors, not mandatory literal illustrations. Do not
force botanical imagery into every component or interaction. Use it when
it adds meaning.

The metaphor should evolve through the journey: - Home introduces the
garden. - Question Garden encourages planting. - Visualise gives
attention to selected seeds. - Gallery presents concepts as a collection
of blossoms or developed ideas. - Compare makes relationships and
branching visible. - Experience provides a calmer place to reflect on
the wider landscape.

Do not imply that every idea must grow, bloom or reach a finished state.
Curiosity is allowed to pause, change direction or remain unresolved.

## 5. Emotional design principles

### 5.1 Warmth --- make people feel welcome

The environment should feel comfortable and inviting. Avoid
intimidating, clinical or overly technical presentation where it is not
needed.

### 5.2 Care --- treat ideas as worth nurturing

The user's questions and interests deserve respectful treatment,
regardless of how unusual, simple or unfinished they may be.

### 5.3 Wonder --- leave room for the unexpected

Discovery should feel possible at every stage. Do not over-explain,
over-structure or remove all ambiguity from the experience.

### 5.4 Connection --- help relationships become visible

Help users notice how ideas relate, while leaving the user in control of
which connections matter.

### 5.5 Agency --- support meaningful action

Help users distinguish what they can control, influence and only be
concerned about. Do not turn this into a score, performance target or
achievement system.

## 6. Visual identity

Preserve and develop the visual language already present in the product.
The existing direction has useful character and should be refined rather
than replaced without a clear reason.

### Core direction

-   **Backgrounds:** warm parchment and natural cream.
-   **Primary accent:** Curitopia orange.
-   **Secondary palette:** sage, moss, natural greens and terracotta.
-   **Typography:** expressive editorial serif paired with a clean,
    readable sans-serif.
-   **Illustration:** organic, tactile and slightly magical, without
    becoming childish.
-   **Concept artwork:** distinctive, evocative imagery that gives each
    idea its own identity.
-   **Cards:** soft edges, considered spacing and a tactile quality.
-   **Animation:** gentle, organic and purposeful.
-   **Navigation:** simple, unobtrusive and consistent.

### Contrast and restraint

-   Use colour to establish atmosphere and hierarchy, not to decorate
    every surface.
-   Preserve readable text contrast and accessible interaction states.
-   Allow artwork to stand out against quieter interface surfaces.
-   Avoid excessive gradients, shadows, ornaments or competing visual
    effects.
-   Do not make every page equally busy. The Question Garden can feel
    playful; Experience should feel more contemplative.

### Existing strengths to preserve

-   The warm cream background and orange branding.
-   The editorial serif typography.
-   The concise, evocative page headlines.
-   The botanical seed and seedling imagery.
-   The dark artwork cards in the Gallery.
-   The organic landscape and contour-line treatment in Experience.
-   Generous breathing room and a relatively uncluttered interface.

## 7. Interaction and motion principles

Interactions should make the environment feel responsive to the user's
thinking. Animation must communicate a change, provide feedback or
create a moment of delight. Do not animate simply because animation is
possible.

Potential interaction language:

  User action                 Possible response
  --------------------------- -------------------------------------
  Creates a curiosity         A seed gently appears
  Asks a question             A new seed is planted
  Selects a question          A seedling emerges
  Saves a concept             A blossom opens
  Connects two concepts       A branch grows between them
  Discovers a new question    A new seed appears in the garden
  Returns to an old concept   Its artwork and history are waiting

These are art-direction possibilities, not instructions to implement all
of them immediately. Choose only interactions that fit the existing
product behaviour and can be implemented accessibly and reliably.

Motion should be: - Subtle rather than theatrical. - Short and
purposeful. - Consistent across the application. - Respectful of
reduced-motion preferences. - Non-blocking: never make the user wait for
decorative animation.

## 8. Language and voice

Curitopia should speak to the user's sense of wonder, not their need to
be productive.

Existing language worth preserving includes: - "Which curiosity shall we
tend?" - "Follow the thread. See what unfolds." - "Give this question a
visual form." - "Concepts worth looking at again." - "A thought, if one
appears." - "There is no set of questions to get through."

Possible language direction:

  Conventional software wording   Curitopia wording
  ------------------------------- --------------------------
  Create new topic                Plant a new curiosity
  Add question                    Plant a new seed
  Saved concepts                  Your collection
  Compare items                   Bring two ideas together
  View history                    Revisit your discoveries
  Delete question                 Let this one go
  Related questions               Follow this thread
  Start again                     Begin a new exploration

Use this vocabulary naturally. Do not force a gardening phrase into
every label. Clarity always takes priority over metaphor.

### AI voice

The AI should feel like an attentive, thoughtful companion: curious,
respectful and context-aware.

It should: - Remember and use relevant context from the whole journey. -
Encourage exploration rather than rush towards closure. - Introduce
alternative perspectives and possible connections. - Treat unusual or
apparently irrelevant questions respectfully. - Recognise shifts in the
user's thinking, not just the accumulation of facts. - Avoid making the
user feel foolish, tested or judged. - Avoid pretending to have human
feelings or personal experiences.

For example, prefer a response that notices an emerging thread over one
that reports a count of questions or labels some as duplicates. Do not
flatter the user indiscriminately; make observations specific and
grounded.

## 9. Five non-negotiable design principles

1.  **Make people feel welcome.** Curiosity must never feel
    intimidating, embarrassing or exclusive.
2.  **Encourage growth, never demand it.** There is no required pace,
    destination or minimum level of engagement.
3.  **Make discovery delightful.** Unexpected connections and new
    interests should feel like discoveries, not interruptions.
4.  **Make relationships visible.** Help people see how ideas connect
    without deciding those connections for them.
5.  **Help people discover their agency.** The value is not simply
    knowing more, but understanding what matters and where they can make
    a difference.

## 10. A test for every screen

When reviewing a screen or interaction, ask:

-   Does it make the user feel welcome?
-   Is its purpose intuitively clear?
-   Does it invite curiosity rather than demand productivity?
-   Does it preserve the user's freedom to follow an unexpected thought?
-   Does it make the user's ideas feel valued?
-   Does it help the user discover, appreciate or connect something?
-   Does it respect the user's agency and ownership?
-   Does it belong visually and emotionally to the same Curitopia world?

If a proposed design change makes the interface more polished but less
welcoming, less permissive or less clear, it is not an improvement.

## 11. Implementation guidance for Cursor

This document is an art-direction brief, not permission to redesign the
application wholesale.

When implementing improvements:

1.  **Work phase by phase.** Only change the phase or components
    explicitly requested in the current task.
2.  **Inspect before changing.** Read the relevant existing components,
    styles, assets and product documentation before proposing
    implementation.
3.  **Preserve product behaviour.** Do not change data models,
    persistence, routes, AI behaviour, business logic or existing
    interactions unless the task explicitly requires it.
4.  **Respect established patterns.** Reuse existing components, design
    tokens and styling conventions where practical. Avoid introducing a
    second design system.
5.  **Keep the experience responsive.** Check the actual mobile and
    desktop layouts. Do not assume a desktop treatment will translate
    well to mobile.
6.  **Preserve the horizontal carousel convention.** Where Curitopia
    presents a list of items, use the established looping, horizontally
    scrolling carousel direction rather than introducing a conventional
    long vertical list. Confirm the intended behaviour and existing
    implementation before changing it.
7.  **Avoid speculative features.** The examples in this brief are
    design opportunities, not a backlog of features to build
    automatically.
8.  **Keep changes focused.** Do not opportunistically refactor
    unrelated code or alter other phases.
9.  **Check accessibility.** Preserve keyboard access, visible focus,
    readable contrast, useful labels and reduced-motion support.
10. **Verify the result.** Run the relevant checks and inspect the
    changed screen at mobile and desktop sizes. Report what changed,
    what was tested and anything left unresolved.

### Suggested command format for each improvement phase

Use this structure when asking Cursor to work on a phase:

> Read `Art-Directors.md` before making changes. We are working on
> **Phase \[number\] --- \[phase name\]**.
>
> **Objective:** \[Describe the specific improvement.\]
>
> **Scope:** \[Name the screen, components or interactions that may
> change.\]
>
> **Preserve:** Existing behaviour and anything that must not change.
>
> **Acceptance criteria:** \[Describe the visible and behavioural
> result.\]
>
> First inspect the existing implementation and report the relevant
> files and proposed approach. Do not modify code until the approach is
> agreed, unless the task explicitly asks you to implement immediately.
> Keep the work limited to this phase.

## 12. The north star for art direction

> **Curitopia should feel like a place where people fall in love with
> discovering things they love and exploring the things they already
> love.**

The application should feel less like building a library of knowledge
and more like building a personal collection of things you love
understanding.

It should make people feel that their curiosity is worth caring about.

That is the emotional foundation for every screen, interaction,
illustration and design decision.
