# Curitopia — Experience Specification

**Status:** Product and UX working specification
**Version:** 0.3
**Updated:** 2026-11-13
**Scope:** Product intent, AI facilitation, user journey, agency model, interaction and visual direction
**Implementation status:** Experience foundation and initial North Star / circle persistence exist in the development environment. The current three-column interface is a functional foundation, not the approved product experience. This specification redirects the next design and implementation phase.

---

## 1. Purpose

Experience helps a person move from being curious about something to recognising what they could do with that curiosity, discovering where their agency lies, and becoming interested in trying something.

Curiosity has already done important work. The person has chosen a subject, explored it from different angles, developed questions, followed connections and perhaps challenged their own assumptions. Experience must build on that investment. It should not greet them with an empty form or ask them to repeat what they already discovered.

The central proposition is:

> **Curiosity gives someone a reason to explore. Understanding their agency reveals opportunities to act. Experimentation gives them a reason to become curious again.**

Experience is not a task-management system, goal tracker or productivity workflow. Its purpose is to help people see possibilities they might not otherwise have noticed, especially possibilities they can explore themselves.

The intended response is not “I have completed my plan.” It is:

> “I hadn't thought of that. I wonder what would happen if I tried it?”

## 2. The product insight and reference prototype

### 2.1 What the successful prototype demonstrated

The reference prototype began with this professional tension, expressed by the user:

> As an IT contractor, your paycheck is as good as the work you produce. You come out of the project's Capex budget; you are a resource as far as the company is concerned. You have been hired for your technical skills, but others have their own way of doing things. When your opinion differs on how something should be done, a delicate balance arises, especially if their ego is tied to their work. There is no need to upset people, but that has to balance with meeting deadlines.

The AI recognised a deeper tension: the person is accountable for delivering technical value but does not necessarily own the environment in which that value must be delivered. It separated the situation into three interacting forces:

- **Technical responsibility:** the person is expected to identify problems, recommend solutions and help deliver the project.
- **Commercial reality:** their value is demonstrated through useful work and delivery, not simply winning technical arguments.
- **Human dynamics:** colleagues may have invested professional identity in their own approaches, so a technical disagreement can feel personal.

It then articulated a useful distinction:

> You may be technically responsible for identifying the best approach without having the authority to decide that it will be adopted.

This interpretation helped make the situation understandable without reducing it to a simplistic story about ego.

### 2.2 North Star synthesis

The AI proposed a direction, rather than a measurable target:

> How can I exercise my professional judgement while navigating different perspectives, maintaining trust and delivering meaningful outcomes?

It elaborated that direction as confidence in expertise without becoming attached to being right; challenging approaches constructively; recognising when to influence rather than insist; and maintaining momentum towards the agreed outcome.

The proposal was grounded in the user's situation and offered as something to refine, not as a verdict about the person.

### 2.3 Agency made concrete

The AI then proposed a starting map.

**Concern** included whether colleagues accept recommendations, whether someone feels challenged, organisational politics and ownership, the project deadline, and how the organisation values the person's contribution.

**Influence** included building trust and credibility, presenting recommendations, helping people understand technical risks, offering alternatives and trade-offs, and influencing decisions through evidence.

**Control** included the quality of technical analysis, clarity of communication, listening before challenging, time management, when and how to escalate a risk, and responding to disagreement.

The key insight was not that the person should stop caring about the outer circle. It was that energy spent on outcomes they cannot determine may be energy unavailable for things they can influence or choose.

### 2.4 An experiment emerged

The AI identified a practical experiment called **The Constructive Challenge**. Rather than avoiding disagreement, the person could try introducing it in a way that protects both the technical outcome and the working relationship:

1. Establish the shared goal: what are we trying to deliver, and by when?
2. Understand the other person's reasoning: what considerations led to this approach?
3. Present the concern: what technical risk or trade-off do I see?
4. Offer alternatives: what else could achieve the same goal?
5. Agree how to decide: who owns the decision, and what evidence do they need?

This did not dilute the person's technical opinion. It made the opinion easier to consider without making the other person feel personally undermined. It also recognised that, if a decision went another way, the person could ensure the risk was understood and documented, then help the team move forward.

The conversation ended by narrowing into a concrete situation. The AI offered several possible scenarios and several possible sources of difficulty, allowing the user to choose the most relevant thread rather than forcing a generic questionnaire.

### 2.5 What we should learn from this run

The successful experience was not a form being completed. It was a sequence of useful cognitive moves carried by a conversation:

- Recognise the deeper tension beneath the user's initial description.
- Make the situation easier to see by separating its interacting forces.
- Offer a tentative synthesis that gives the user language for what they are experiencing.
- Propose a North Star that captures a direction without turning it into a target.
- Translate the situation into a first agency map.
- Notice an opportunity for experimentation.
- Make that experiment concrete enough to imagine trying.
- Open a more specific line of exploration without losing the wider thread.

The AI did meaningful intellectual work. It did not merely transcribe the user's words into three categories. **This is the reference behaviour for Experience.**

The prototype is evidence of a promising interaction, not proof that every interpretation was correct or that every user will follow the same path. The user remains the authority on what resonates and what they choose to adopt.

## 3. Product principles

### 3.1 Curiosity is the source of motivation

Do not assume the person needs to be motivated, persuaded or pushed into action. Their sustained exploration is already evidence of interest. Help them notice what that interest makes possible.

### 3.2 AI should do meaningful facilitation work

AI is a central capability of Experience, not a decorative suggestion button. It should actively help the person interpret, connect, reframe, synthesise, map agency and discover experiments. It should take initiative when a useful pattern or opportunity emerges, while remaining tentative and corrigible.

### 3.3 The user owns meaning and decisions

The AI may offer interpretations, candidate North Stars, map items and experiments. It must distinguish its hypotheses from what the user has actually said or confirmed. The user can accept, edit, reject, defer or ignore every proposal. No personal interpretation or proposed map change is silently saved as the user's belief or intention.

### 3.4 Reveal agency, including its limits

Help the person distinguish:

- what matters to them but is outside their direct control;
- where their actions can affect or shape an outcome;
- what they can directly choose, do, practise or manage.

Do not imply that sufficient effort can overcome every external constraint, another person's autonomy, chance or structural condition.

### 3.5 Agency can grow

A person's influence is not fixed. Knowledge, experience, skill, relationships, credibility, access, resources and authority can expand what they can influence or control. An expert may have a much larger sphere of influence in a subject than someone encountering it for the first time. Experience should help the person see how that growth might happen, without promising that it will.

### 3.6 Experimentation is discovery, not task completion

An experiment is a way to learn, test an assumption, experience something, develop a capability or discover what is possible. It is not a task with a pass/fail result. A surprising or disappointing outcome can still be valuable learning.

### 3.7 Possibility, not obligation

The interface should invite exploration and make possibilities compelling, but never create pressure to act, finish, improve or report back. A person may simply reflect and leave with a different understanding.

### 3.8 Preserve the thread and allow tangents

Maintain the original Curiosity and the current focus, but do not trap the person inside them. Useful tangents and unexpected connections are welcome. Keep enough context to return to the original thread.

### 3.9 Non-linear and revisable

A person may revisit any part of Experience, change direction, move an agency item inward or outward, or pause. There is no required sequence and no completion state.

### 3.10 Curiosity does not have to become productive

Some interests are valuable simply because they are interesting, enjoyable or absorbing. Experience is optional and must not imply that a curiosity is incomplete until it produces an outcome.

## 4. The role of AI in Experience

### 4.1 AI as thinking partner and facilitator

The AI should feel like an attentive, perceptive thinking partner: curious about the user's subject, able to notice patterns, willing to offer a useful interpretation, and comfortable being corrected.

It should be more proactive than a passive chatbot waiting for instructions. It can say, for example:

- “I think there's a deeper tension here.”
- “These three things seem to be operating at once.”
- “I wonder if this is less about avoiding disagreement and more about exercising judgement without owning the final decision.”
- “There may be an experiment hiding in this.”
- “You may not be able to determine that outcome, but you could influence the conditions around it.”

These are invitations to think, not claims of authority.

### 4.2 Adaptive conversation

- Ask one primary question at a time.
- Adapt to the user's actual answer rather than following a fixed script.
- Use short sets of options when they make it easier to recognise or choose a direction; always allow a free-form answer or “none of these”.
- Avoid long questionnaires and repeated requests for information already supplied.
- Offer synthesis when enough context has emerged, not after every message.
- Make a useful observation or proposal before asking another question when the conversation has earned one.
- Allow the user to move on without answering every question.
- Do not mechanically march through Gather, Discover, Imagine, Map, Expand, Experiment and Reflect.

### 4.3 Interpretation and synthesis

The AI should:

1. Identify the important facts and tensions in the user's description.
2. Separate interacting forces where doing so improves understanding.
3. Notice patterns across the current conversation and relevant Curiosity source material.
4. Offer a concise synthesis in language that preserves the user's meaning.
5. State uncertainty where a connection is speculative.
6. Invite correction or ask whether the interpretation resonates.
7. Refine the synthesis in response to the user.

It should not over-psychologise, diagnose, make authoritative dream interpretations or invent a coherent story where the evidence is thin.

### 4.4 North Star support

The AI should help a North Star emerge from what matters to the user. It may propose one or more formulations when there is enough context. A North Star is a direction, not a SMART goal, KPI, deadline or contract. It can be provisional, revised, replaced or left undefined.

The North Star should help orient exploration and experiments, but it must not become a constraint that prevents valuable tangents.

### 4.5 Agency-map support

The AI should do more than ask the user to populate empty circles. It should:

- identify candidate concerns, influences and controllable choices from the conversation;
- explain why an item might sit in a particular circle;
- distinguish an outcome from the actions or conditions that can affect it;
- notice where the person may be spending energy on an outcome they cannot determine;
- identify opportunities to redirect attention towards influence or control;
- ask whether the proposed map reflects the user's experience;
- help the user revise placements as their understanding changes.

The user confirms what becomes part of their persistent map. Classification is contextual, not an objective judgement by the AI.

### 4.6 Experiment discovery

The AI should actively look for experiments suggested by the user's own curiosities, insights, concerns, capabilities and circumstances. It should connect a proposed experiment to its source: “This might be worth trying because you were curious about…”

Good experiments may be:

- small and immediately approachable;
- exploratory, playful, practical or creative;
- a conversation, observation, prototype, rehearsal, comparison, investigation or deliberate change in approach;
- designed to test an assumption or develop a skill or relationship;
- useful even if the desired outcome does not occur.

The AI should explain what the experiment could reveal, not just prescribe an action. It may offer alternatives with different levels of effort or exposure. The user chooses whether to try anything and explicitly confirms any experiment saved as an intention.

Do not pressure the user to contact someone, disclose information, take professional or personal risks, spend money or accept consequences. Consider safety, privacy, power dynamics and reversibility when proposing experiments.

### 4.7 Learning and agency growth

After an experiment or new experience, AI can help the user notice:

- what happened versus what was expected;
- what they learned about the subject or situation;
- what new skill, relationship, resource, access or credibility they gained;
- whether something previously outside their influence is now more influenceable;
- whether their understanding of a boundary has become more realistic;
- what new curiosity has emerged.

The AI may suggest that an item has changed circles, but must ask the user to confirm the interpretation and placement.

### 4.8 Evidence and authorship

Keep these distinctions clear in conversation and persistence:

- **Source material:** questions, concepts or context selected from Curiosity.
- **User statement:** something the person explicitly said.
- **AI hypothesis:** a proposed interpretation or connection.
- **Candidate synthesis:** an AI-organised summary awaiting user response.
- **User-confirmed insight:** wording the user explicitly accepted or edited.
- **Candidate map item / experiment:** a proposal not yet adopted.
- **User-adopted map item / experiment:** content the user chose to keep.

AI must not silently overwrite, reclassify or persist user content.

## 5. Core concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | A broad area of interest containing questions and concepts. |
| **Source material** | Existing questions, concepts and other user-selected context that inform an Experience. |
| **Thread** | A theme or line of exploration connecting material. It is optional and user-confirmed. |
| **Focus situation** | A current challenge, decision, opportunity or circumstance the person wants to understand. |
| **North Star** | An aspirational direction that matters to the user; optional and revisable. |
| **Agency landscape** | A visual representation of what concerns the person, where they can influence outcomes and what they can directly control. |
| **Agency item** | A factor, condition, relationship, capability, resource, action or outcome relevant to the landscape. |
| **Experiment** | A deliberate, bounded attempt to learn, test an assumption or discover what is possible. |
| **Reflection** | A person's account of what happened, what they learned and what they may explore next. |

A Thread, a Focus situation and a North Star are different things. They may inform one another but must not be conflated.

## 6. Experience journey

The following capabilities describe the work Experience supports, not mandatory screens or a linear wizard.

### 6.1 Reconnect with curiosity

Bring relevant questions, concepts, comparisons and discoveries into the conversation. Do not make the user restate context already available. AI may identify potentially relevant material, but the user decides what belongs in the current exploration.

The person may also start from a current situation without prior Curiosity material. The experience must support both entry routes.

### 6.2 Understand what is happening

Help the user articulate the situation, tension, opportunity or subject. The AI may separate interacting forces, surface assumptions, identify contradictions and offer a useful synthesis. The user can correct the framing.

### 6.3 Imagine a direction

Explore what the user would like to understand, experience, create, discover or contribute. A North Star may emerge, but is never required. The direction should create orientation and possibility rather than pressure or a performance target.

### 6.4 Reveal the agency landscape

Make visible:

- **Concern:** what matters to the person but whose outcome they cannot determine.
- **Influence:** where their actions can affect or shape an outcome, without guaranteeing it.
- **Control:** what they can directly choose, do, practise or manage.

These are not three task lists. They are lenses for understanding a situation and where energy might usefully go.

The AI proposes a starting interpretation from the conversation. The user corrects and confirms it. The landscape should show relationships and opportunities, not merely store disconnected notes.

### 6.5 Notice opportunities to experiment

This is the pivotal transition. As the person sees where their agency lies, Curitopia should help them notice what they could try. Existing curiosities are a primary source of inspiration: a question, concept, comparison or tangent may suggest an experiment that would not otherwise have occurred to them.

The AI should make the connection explicit. It might surface:

- a small action already within the person's control;
- an experiment that could expand their influence;
- an assumption worth testing;
- a conversation or observation that could reveal something;
- a capability or relationship they could develop;
- a new possibility created by combining two of their earlier curiosities.

The goal is not to generate a large list. One compelling possibility is better than twenty generic suggestions.

### 6.6 Experiment

Help the person shape an experiment in their own language. Useful prompts may include:

- What are you curious to find out?
- What could you try?
- What might you notice or learn?
- What would make this experiment interesting, even if it doesn't work as expected?

A saved experiment may record its question, proposed action and learning intention. Timing, people, resources and notes are optional. Do not introduce task status, due dates, priorities, reminders, completion percentages or streaks as default requirements.

### 6.7 Reflect and return to curiosity

After the experiment, help the person explore what happened and what it means. They may revise the agency landscape, change the North Star, try another experiment, return to an earlier question, create a new Curiosity question or simply stop.

The loop is:

**Curiosity → Understanding → Agency → Possibility → Experiment → Discovery → New Curiosity**

This is a living cycle, not a prescribed sequence.

## 7. Agency model: Concern, Influence and Control

### 7.1 The circles are a thinking model, not the interface metaphor

Concern, Influence and Control remain important concepts. However, presenting them as three rigid columns of editable cards makes Experience look like task management. That is not the intended product experience.

The circles should inform a more organic, connected visual landscape. The user should feel they are looking at a situation and discovering openings within it, not filing information into three containers.

### 7.2 Circle definitions

**Concern — What matters to me, but whose outcome I cannot determine.**

Examples include other people's choices, external events, organisational constraints, market conditions, deadlines and uncertain outcomes.

**Influence — Where my actions can affect or shape the outcome, but cannot guarantee it.**

Examples include relationships, communication, persuasion, collaboration, reputation, access to decision-makers and the ability to shape a process.

**Control — What I can directly choose, do, practise or manage.**

Examples include preparation, learning, personal responses, decisions, communication choices, practice and actions within the user's authority.

The user decides where an item belongs. The AI can suggest and explain alternatives, but must not present placement as objectively certain.

### 7.3 Agency is contextual and can grow

The same subject may sit in different circles for different people. A newcomer may be able to control their own learning and influence a few local interactions. An expert may have developed the knowledge, credibility, relationships, resources and authority to influence much broader outcomes.

Agency can change as the person:

- learns how a system works;
- develops knowledge or skill;
- practises and gains confidence;
- builds relationships and trust;
- gains access, resources or authority;
- discovers a different approach;
- experiences a change in circumstances.

The landscape should allow items to move in either direction. Movement inward is not a score or reward. Movement outward is not failure; it may reflect a more accurate understanding or a new constraint.

### 7.4 Energy and attention

A key purpose of the model is to help people notice when too much energy is being spent on outcomes they cannot determine. Curitopia should not tell people to stop caring about those things. It should help them decide where attention and effort may be more useful, including actions that can influence the situation or choices they can make directly.

### 7.5 Relationships between items

The map should support meaningful relationships between concerns, influences, controllable actions and possible experiments. For example, a concern about whether colleagues accept a recommendation may connect to controllable choices about listening and clarity, and influence opportunities around trust, evidence and how alternatives are presented.

The purpose of visual connections is to reveal leverage and possibility, not to create a complex knowledge graph for its own sake.

## 8. Visual and interaction direction

### 8.1 Desired feeling

Experience should feel like looking across a landscape of possibilities. It should be calm, open, exploratory and alive with potential. It should communicate that ideas can connect, grow and lead somewhere unexpected.

The current three-column presentation, with repeated cards and permanent Edit / Move / Remove controls, communicates administration and task management. It is a useful data-entry prototype but is not the desired visual direction.

### 8.2 Visual principles

- Treat the agency model as an interconnected landscape rather than three filing columns.
- Make the user's current direction visible without making it look like a business objective.
- Give potential experiments visual prominence as openings or invitations, not assigned tasks.
- Show relationships between a curiosity, a concern, an influence and an experiment where those relationships are meaningful.
- Use whitespace to preserve a sense of possibility; do not fill the screen with every available item.
- Keep the experience visually coherent with Curitopia's established design system: warm, editorial, organic and thoughtful.
- Avoid childish gardening-game aesthetics, mystical claims, gamification, progress dashboards and excessive metaphor.
- Use plain language for controls even when the surrounding visual language is expressive.

### 8.3 Interaction principles

- Conversation is the primary way to discover and develop meaning.
- The visual landscape is a persistent companion to the conversation, not a report generated at the end.
- The AI can propose a synthesis, relationship, map item or experiment in context.
- The user can accept, edit, reject or defer a proposal.
- Editing controls should appear when relevant, not dominate every item all the time.
- Do not require dragging; provide accessible tap, click and keyboard alternatives.
- Do not make the user manually populate every circle before the AI can help.
- Do not turn an experiment into a task card with status, deadline and completion state.
- The user can return to the conversation and the map without losing context.

### 8.4 Responsive and accessible design

Follow the existing Curitopia design system. Support desktop and mobile, keyboard navigation, visible focus, accessible names, sufficient contrast and non-colour distinctions. On mobile, prioritise readable content and natural conversational flow over forcing a dense map into a narrow viewport.

## 9. Data, persistence and implementation boundaries

This section records product requirements and current implementation context. It is not approval for additional SQL, migrations or schema changes.

### 9.1 Existing implementation foundation

The current development implementation already provides:

- one Experience associated with each Curiosity;
- an optional `north_star` value on `experiences`;
- persistent `experience_circle_items` with Concern, Influence and Control categories;
- owner-scoped access and RLS protections;
- a development-only migration path established separately from production.

These are useful foundations. They do not dictate the final interface or prove that the current card-and-column interaction is right.

### 9.2 Product requirements for future persistence

As the design evolves, assess whether persistence needs to represent:

- conversation turns or a privacy-conscious conversation summary;
- source links to existing questions and concepts;
- user-confirmed synthesis and its provenance;
- AI hypotheses separately from confirmed insights;
- relationships between agency items;
- candidate versus user-adopted experiments;
- experiment observations and reflections;
- changes to agency understanding over time.

Do not add all of these pre-emptively. Prototype the conversational and visual experience first, then design the smallest data model that supports demonstrated needs.

### 9.3 Ownership, privacy and AI context

- Experiences and their content remain private to their owner.
- Enforce ownership and access server-side using the existing Supabase architecture and RLS.
- Do not expose service-role credentials to the browser.
- Assemble AI context from the current curiosity, every concept the person developed for it and the provenance of those concepts, the confirmed Experience content, and the current conversation. Questions that were not developed into concepts are not part of that context.
- Avoid sending unrelated personal content to the model.
- Make clear which content is retained and what the user can edit or delete.
- Do not persist speculative AI interpretations as user facts.

### 9.4 Safe implementation sequence

- Treat this specification as a product and UX direction, not a migration request.
- Audit the current branch, components, routes, data access and tests before implementation.
- Preserve the existing Phase 1 / Phase 2 foundation unless a reviewed change is necessary.
- Work against the development Supabase project only.
- Do not change production environment configuration, production schema or production data.
- Do not run database migrations or `db push` without a separately reviewed and explicitly approved migration plan.
- Do not introduce Docker or a local Supabase stack; the established workflow uses hosted development Supabase.

## 10. Reference journey: Professional judgement and constructive challenge

This is the reference case for designing and evaluating Experience. It is based on the successful prototype conversation in `Original_test_md`.

### Starting curiosity

The person is an IT contractor whose work is funded from a project budget. They have been hired for technical skills, but must navigate other people's established ways of working. They need to offer sound technical judgement without damaging relationships or losing delivery momentum.

### Useful AI moves

1. Recognise the tension between technical responsibility, commercial reality and human dynamics.
2. Offer the distinction between identifying the best technical approach and having authority to decide whether it is adopted.
3. Propose a North Star about exercising professional judgement while navigating perspectives, maintaining trust and delivering meaningful outcomes.
4. Suggest candidate items across Concern, Influence and Control.
5. Explain that the point is not to stop caring about concerns, but to redirect energy towards areas where the person can influence or choose.
6. Discover The Constructive Challenge as a possible experiment.
7. Offer a concrete structure for trying it in a real disagreement.
8. Invite the person to explore a specific situation or the aspect they find hardest.

### What the interface should make possible

- See the North Star as an orienting direction.
- Understand the three interacting forces in the situation.
- See concerns connected to possible influence and controllable responses.
- Notice The Constructive Challenge as an inviting experiment, with its rationale and source context.
- Return to the conversation to refine or reject the proposal.
- Keep the wider professional curiosity available while exploring a specific incident.

### Success signal

The person leaves with a clearer understanding of the situation and an experiment they genuinely want to try—not merely a populated map.

## 11. Success criteria

Experience is working when:

- The person feels the AI has understood the substance of their situation or curiosity, not merely repeated their words.
- AI synthesis is useful, grounded and easy to correct.
- The person recognises a distinction or possibility they had not previously seen.
- Existing Curiosity material meaningfully informs the Experience rather than being abandoned at the boundary between phases.
- The person can distinguish concern, influence and control without feeling judged or blamed.
- The person recognises that agency depends on context and can grow through learning, practice, relationships, resources and experience.
- The visual landscape reveals relationships and opportunities rather than resembling a task board.
- At least one experiment, when appropriate, feels personally relevant and interesting enough that the person wants to try it.
- The person understands what the experiment might help them discover, even if it does not produce the hoped-for result.
- The person remains free to reject every suggestion, pause or leave without a sense of failure.
- Reflection can generate new questions and renewed curiosity.

Do not measure success by the number of cards, map movements, tasks or experiments completed. Do not equate more action with more growth.

## 12. Deferred decisions

The following should be resolved through design and implementation discovery, not assumed now:

1. The final visual metaphor and geometry for the agency landscape.
2. How much conversation is retained versus a concise approved synthesis.
3. Whether the user can select multiple source curiosities within one Experience, and how this relates to the existing one-Experience-per-Curiosity foundation.
4. How AI proposals are presented, accepted, edited and rejected in the visual workspace.
5. Whether experiments are represented as connected objects, visual invitations or another lightweight form.
6. How agency-item relationships and movement history are represented without creating a complex graph.
7. How much of the AI conversation should be visible alongside the map on desktop and mobile.
8. Which data needs to be persisted after the redesigned prototype is validated.

## 13. Recommended next steps

1. Use the successful professional-judgement conversation as the canonical reference journey.
2. Redesign the Experience interaction and visual concept around conversational AI facilitation and the agency-to-experiment transition.
3. Prototype the landscape and its connection to the conversation before changing the database.
4. Evaluate whether the prototype makes The Constructive Challenge feel discoverable, relevant and inviting—not assigned.
5. Only after the interaction is convincing, define any required changes to persistence, AI orchestration and application architecture.

## 14. Guiding principle

> **Curitopia should help people discover what they could do, until the possibility of trying becomes more compelling than the comfort of simply wondering.**

Curiosity supplies the interest. AI helps reveal patterns and possibilities. The agency landscape helps people recognise where they can act and where their influence might grow. Experimentation turns that possibility into experience. The user decides what matters and what to try.
